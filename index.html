🌸 The West Point Honor Process

An interactive, step-by-step guide from the first accusation all the way through final Superintendent adjudication, separation, retention, and SLDP-H.

Phase 1

1. A potential violation occurs

Someone becomes aware of conduct that may violate the Cadet Honor Code: “A Cadet will not lie, cheat, steal, or tolerate those who do.”

Potential violations can be reported by Cadets, Staff, Faculty, and certain personnel outside West Point who are involved with Cadets.

Phase 1 continued

2. Approach for clarification / notification

The purpose is initially to figure out what happened—not to automatically accuse the Cadet.

What if the situation is explained?
If the explanation resolves the concern, there is no Honor case.
What if suspicion remains?
Once the person believes an Honor violation may have occurred, questioning must stop and the Cadet must be advised of Article 31 rights before further questioning.
What are the Cadet's choices?
The Cadet can remain silent or seek legal assistance. If the Cadet waives the applicable rights and continues talking, questioning can continue under the required procedures.
💡 Memory trick: Clarify first. Once it becomes an actual suspected violation, rights kick in.
Phase 2

3. Case inception

The case enters the Honor System. The RHR officially incepts the case by notifying the Cadet that they are a Cadet Under Investigation (CUI) and assigning an Investigative Team (IT).

The IT comes from the CUI's regiment but not the CUI's company.

4. Investigative Team (IT)

The IT investigates the allegation and gathers evidence that supports or does not support the alleged violation.

📝 Sworn statements
👥 Witness testimony
📄 Documents
💬 Emails / texts
🎥 Video / audio
📚 Academic materials
Electronic evidence has special procedures. Before an IT requests inspection of a personal electronic device, the Cadet must be advised of applicable Article 31 rights; consent/search authorization rules then apply.

When finished, the IT sends the RHR the investigation, summary memorandum, timeline, evidence summary, pertinent issues, and recommended allegations.

5. RHR review

The Regimental Honor Representative reviews the completed investigation.

FORWARDProbable cause exists for each element.
DROPAn element has no supporting evidence.
RETURNThe investigation needs more work.

6. HIO review

The Honor Investigations Officer independently reviews the evidence and recommendations.

FORWARDProbable cause for each required element.
DROPNo evidence supports an element.
RETURNMore investigation is needed.
If RHR and HIO disagree: the case can go to the Honor Captain for resolution.

7. SAH → BJA → Commandant

SAH: checks the thoroughness/procedural sufficiency and sends a forwarded case for legal review.

BJA: conducts the legal sufficiency review and prepares the pre-referral legal advice.

Commandant: reviews the complete record and is the only authority authorized to refer a case to an Honor hearing.

Commandant choices: REFER • RETURN for additional investigation • DROP/DISMISS.
Phase 3

8. The Cadet is served

If referred, the HSEC serves the Respondent with notification of referral, the complete investigation packet, relevant evidence, and notice of rights/responsibilities.

9. Respondent rights

10. Admit or contest?

ADMIT → CABA formal admission must cover all elements and be accepted by the Hearing Advisor.
CONTEST → HIHContested allegations go to an Honor Investigative Hearing.

11. Preliminary hearing

The preliminary hearing addresses rights, objections, evidence, witnesses, procedural issues, and—if applicable—a formal admission.

The Respondent receives time to meet with a Legal Assistance attorney and prepare. For an HIH, a second preliminary hearing normally occurs the morning of the hearing to finalize the board packet, witness list, and evidence.

12. Board & hearing roles

Board PresidentRuns the board and maintains order.
Hearing AdvisorProvides legal/procedural guidance.
RespondentThe Cadet whose case is being heard.
Cadet AdvisorAssists/advises the Respondent.
IRPresents the investigation/evidence.
Legal TechnicianSupports recording, evidence, and witness coordination.

13. Voir dire

Board members are questioned for impartiality. The Respondent and IR can challenge voting board members for cause.

14. HIH evidence & testimony

The board reviews the approved case packet. The IR and Respondent can make opening statements. Witness testimony and evidence are then presented.

The Respondent has the opportunity to question witnesses, present witnesses/evidence, and respond to the allegations.

15. Closing statements & deliberation

After testimony, the IR and Respondent give closing statements. The board then enters closed deliberations.

Important: During deliberations, the board does not receive new evidence or testimony. It decides the allegations from the evidence admitted during the hearing.

16. Finding

For an HIH, the standard of proof is preponderance of the evidence—more likely than not, or greater than 50%.

The nine voting members vote by secret written ballot on each allegation. 6 of 9 votes are required for a FOUND finding.

❌ NOT FOUNDThe case is closed. The administrative flag is closed.
✅ FOUNDThe case proceeds to post-hearing review and adjudication.
Phase 5

17. Immediate actions after FOUND

The SAH speaks with the Cadet and, within 48 hours, addresses the FOUND flag, imposed consequences, privacy authorization, and company assignment request.

18. The review & recommendation process

The formal review process begins with a FOUND HIH or completion of a CAB and ends with the Superintendent's final adjudication.

19. Record of proceedings

A summarized transcript is produced. The Hearing Advisor—or, when applicable, the legal technician or Board President—authenticates the accuracy and certifies the findings/recommendations.

20. Honor Captain recommendation

The Honor Captain reviews the completed case file and makes a recommendation for final adjudication.

21. SAH recommendation

The SAH reviews the case and provides a written recommendation, summary of violations, timeline, and summary of board recommendations.

22. Chain of Command recommendations

Company and tactical leadership provide recommendations concerning final disposition to the Commandant.

23. Commandant's Honor meeting

The Commandant normally meets with the FOUND Cadet. Appropriate leadership and Honor personnel may attend, but someone who reported the Cadet or was directly involved in the alleged violation cannot attend.

After the meeting, the Commandant makes a written recommendation to the Superintendent concerning final disposition.

24. Cadet rebuttal

The Cadet receives the relevant recommendations and record and can submit a written rebuttal and additional matters to the Superintendent. The Commandant's meeting is treated as Day Zero; the Cadet normally has three duty days for this response unless the time is waived.

25. SJA review

After the rebuttal, the Staff Judge Advocate reviews legal issues raised by the Cadet or counsel and prepares the record for Superintendent action.

Final disposition

26. Superintendent adjudication ⭐

The Superintendent reviews the record, evidence, findings, recommendations, legal review, and the Cadet's rebuttal/additional matters.

HIH NOT FOUNDThe Superintendent is bound by the NOT FOUND finding; the case is closed.
FOUNDThe Superintendent evaluates whether the evidence supports the finding and determines final disposition.

27. Superintendent sets aside FOUND

If the Superintendent determines the finding is not supported, the finding can be set aside. The Superintendent may close the case, direct SLDP-H, or direct further investigation as authorized.

28. Superintendent confirms FOUND

29. Separation

The Superintendent may direct separation for Third and Fourth Class Cadets. For First and Second Class Cadets, the Superintendent recommends separation to the appropriate Army authority.

Once separated, only the Secretary of the Army or appointed designee may reinstate a Cadet.

30. Discretion / retention

The Superintendent can retain the Cadet through discretion and impose developmental alternatives. Examples include turning the Cadet back to the next graduating class or delaying graduation.

If retained through discretion, the Cadet is placed in suspended separation and directed to complete SLDP-H.

31. SLDP-H & suspended separation

SLDP-H = Special Leader Development Program–Honor. A Developmental Coach guides the Cadet through structured reflection and introspection aimed at moral/ethical growth.

Successful completion: the Cadet reaches the intended developmental end state and the Superintendent approves completion.
Failure to complete SLDP-H: the Superintendent may vacate the separation suspension and separate the Cadet.

Suspended separation can also be vacated for certain later events, including a second Honor violation, failure to complete SLDP-H, certain misconduct findings, specified demerit thresholds, or an MD “F” while under suspended separation.

Administrative consequences

FOUND Cadets can also face administrative consequences, including restrictions involving athletic/club representation, club trips, Semester Abroad, Academy Exchange, Brigade Open Finals, and public relations activities. Certain consequences can remain in effect until SLDP-H completion or an approved ETP.

ETP: Exception to Policy. Requests are made in writing and routed through the appropriate chain; approval authority depends on the consequence.
⭐ Special situations

Situations that work a little differently

These are the exceptions or special circumstances worth knowing separately from the normal Honor process.

🆕 New Cadets

New Cadets are subject to the Honor Code after taking the oath on R-Day, but they do not receive the normal separation/consequence structure used for Fourth Class Cadets.

If a New Cadet admits
The case goes to a CAB.
If a New Cadet contests
The case goes to a HIH using the standard Fourth Class board.
If a New Cadet is NOT FOUND
The case is closed.
If a New Cadet is FOUND
The New Cadet completes SLDP-H only; standard separation and administrative consequences do not apply while the Cadet is a New Cadet.
Important: After Acceptance Day, the Cadet becomes a Fourth Class Cadet and the standard Honor consequences apply.

⚖️ Honor vs. disciplinary cases

Honor and disciplinary/misconduct cases can run separately or simultaneously. The underlying misconduct can be disciplinary while lying about it can create a separate Honor allegation.

🧩 Multiple allegations

A case may contain multiple allegations. The Commandant can refer all or only some. If the Respondent admits some but contests others, contested allegations go to HIH and admitted allegations can go to CAB.

📚 Academic Honor

The Dean has authority for final approval of an F grade resulting from an Honor violation.

📱 Electronic evidence

Before an IT asks to inspect a Cadet's personal electronic device, applicable Article 31 rights must be addressed. Consent or proper search authorization is then required.

🛡️ Article 31 during clarification

If an approach for clarification changes into suspicion of a violation, questioning must stop and Article 31 rights must be advised before further questioning.

🔄 Further investigation

During review, the RHR, HIO, SAH, or Commandant can direct or request additional investigation when the record needs more work.

❌ NOT FOUND

A NOT FOUND HIH finding closes the Honor case. The administrative flag is closed.

✅ FOUND does not automatically mean separation

A FOUND case moves into post-hearing review and Superintendent adjudication. The Superintendent can confirm the finding, set it aside, or choose separation or discretion/retention as authorized by the publication.

⭐ Five things most worth memorizing

  1. Commandant = referral authority for an Honor hearing.
  2. Contest → HIH. Admit → CAB after the formal admission is accepted.
  3. HIH standard = preponderance of the evidence.
  4. 6 of 9 votes = FOUND on an HIH allegation.
  5. Superintendent = final adjudication. FOUND does not automatically equal separation.

Yes → continue toward Commandant referral.
